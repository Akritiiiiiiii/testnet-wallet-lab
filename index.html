This lab uses an Ethereum test network, where ETH is free and worth nothing. The wallet is built in your browser. The ledger you check at the end is public and lives outside this page, so anyone can look at your transaction.

Testnet only. The key you make here stays in this page and is never sent anywhere. Even so, keep the habit: never paste a private key or recovery phrase from a wallet that holds real money into any web page.

1

Make a wallet

A wallet isn't a container. It's a secret number plus the math that turns it into a public address. Coins never sit inside it. The ledger records what each address owns, and only the secret number can spend it.

2

Get test ETH

A faucet is a website that gives test ETH to any address you paste in. That works because addresses are public: anyone can send to one without asking its owner.

Open address on explorer

Copy your address, pick a faucet, paste it in, and wait a minute or two. Faucets change often, and most limit you to one request a day. If one asks for a real balance or payment, close it and try another.

    Then open your address on the explorer. Your balance shows up there, not on this page, because this page has no connection to the network. The ledger lives somewhere else.

    Sign a payment

    A transaction is a short instruction: send this much to that address. Your signature proves the instruction came from your key, and changing any part of a signed transaction breaks it. Signing happens here in your browser, without touching the network.

    Any address works. The default is a well-known dead address, so the coins are gone for good, which is fine for test coins. Use a classmate's address if you'd like to see it arrive.

    4

    Broadcast and find it

    Signing sends nothing. To make the payment real, hand the signed bytes to any node on the network. Etherscan has a form for exactly that. The node passes it along, and a block proposer includes it in the next block.

    1. Copy the signed transaction from step 3.
    2. Open the broadcast page, paste it in, and press the broadcast button.
    3. Paste your hash below and open it on the explorer.

    Open transaction on explorer
    The explorer says something is wrong

    "Insufficient funds": the faucet ETH hasn't arrived yet, or the amount plus the maximum fee is more than your balance. Check the address page, or lower the amount.

    "Nonce too low": your wallet already sent a transaction with that nonce. Raise the nonce by one and sign again.

    Max fee below the base fee: raise the max fee per gas and sign again.

    "Transaction hash not found": normal for the first minute. The node hasn't shared it widely yet. Refresh.

    Pending for a long time: the fees are probably too low for a busy moment. Sign again with the same nonce and higher fees; only one of the two can ever be included.

    Reading the explorer page

    Every line on the transaction page is either something you chose in step 3 or something the network added when it put your payment in a block. Labels differ a little between explorers.

    You chose these

    From
    Recovered from your signature.
    To
    The address you typed.
    Value
    The amount, stored as whole units of wei (1 ETH is 1018 wei).
    Nonce
    Your count of earlier transactions.
    Gas limit
    The most gas you allowed. 21,000 for a plain transfer.
    Max fee and priority fee
    The most you'd pay per unit of gas, and the tip you offered.

    The network filled these in

    Status
    Pending, then Success once it's in a block.
    Block
    Which block holds it. Click through to see its neighbours.
    Timestamp
    When that block was made.
    Gas used
    What the transfer actually consumed.
    Gas price paid
    The block's base fee plus your tip, at most your max fee.
    Transaction fee
    Gas used times the price paid. Usually less than the maximum you allowed.

    Lab record

    Fill in what you see on the explorer, then copy the record to hand in. It never includes your private key.

    5

    Try to edit a ledger

    Why trust a ledger nobody owns? Each block carries a fingerprint (a hash) of the block before it, and thousands of nodes keep their own copies. Edit the contents of any block below and watch what happens.

    Real Ethereum blocks fingerprint a header that commits to their transactions, not raw text like this. The effect is the same: one edit ripples through every later block.

    6

    Check yourself

    Answer each one in your head, then open it.

    You lose your private key but still know your address. Can you get your coins back?

    No. The address can't be turned back into the key, and no company holds a copy. The ledger still shows the balance, but nobody can sign a payment to move it.

    Why is the chain ID part of what you sign?

    So the signature only works on one network. Without it, someone could take your signed testnet payment and replay it on mainnet, where it might move real money.

    Anyone can read your address's full history. What does that mean for privacy?

    Addresses are pseudonymous, not anonymous. Once an address is linked to you (through an exchange account, a name service, or a payment to a friend), its whole past and future activity is linked too.

    What does the nonce protect against?

    It orders your transactions and makes each one unique. The network accepts nonce 0, then 1, then 2, so the same signed payment can't be executed twice.

    The faucet gave you coins without asking who you are. What stops people draining it?

    Limits on requests per address, per network location, or per account, plus the fact that test coins have no value worth hoarding. Some faucets make you spend computing power to earn them.

    Keys and signatures are made with ethers.js 5.7.2 using your browser's secure random number generator. Nothing typed on this page is sent to a server. Test networks are retired on a schedule, so expect the network menu to change over time.
